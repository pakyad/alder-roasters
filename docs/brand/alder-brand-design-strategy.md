# ALDER ROASTERS — Brand & Design Strategy

**Status:** Phase 2 strategic baseline

**Depends on:** [Phase 1 Product Requirements Document](../product/alder-roasters-prd.md)

**Scope:** Brand strategy, visual system, art direction and interaction principles only
**Not included:** Wireframes, page design, UI components or production code

## Executive direction

ALDER should not look like “another tasteful coffee shop.” Brown, cream, serif typography and grain are category ingredients, not an identity. Its distinctive position is the combination of exact product information and a low-pressure, sensory buying experience.

The organising idea is:

> **Quietly exact.**

This is an internal brand idea, not necessarily the public tagline. It describes a company that is precise about sourcing, roasting and brewing without turning expertise into performance. Visually, it means disciplined information, tactile imagery, measured typography and moments of warmth. In UX, it means making every consequential choice clear while removing unnecessary noise.

The primary strategic tension is **precision × warmth**. Either side alone fails:

- Precision without warmth becomes laboratory-like, elitist and emotionally remote.
- Warmth without precision becomes generic craft nostalgia and cannot justify premium pricing.

### Decisions resolved in this phase

- **Fictional setting:** ALDER is based in Petaling Jaya, Malaysia, with one roastery and tasting room in a converted 1970s joinery workshop. This gives the brand a credible contemporary Southeast Asian point of view and prevents it from defaulting to borrowed Scandinavian or Pacific Northwest imagery.
- **Market and currency:** Malaysian direct-to-consumer market; prices in MYR. English is the launch language, with a future Malay-language path considered but not simulated in MVP.
- **Core audience:** Quality-seeking home brewers who value good coffee but dislike specialist gatekeeping.
- **Creative territory:** Contemporary editorial utility with workshop materiality—not faux heritage, rustic café décor or clinical Nordic minimalism.
- **Typography direction:** Licensed editorial serif plus neutral, highly functional sans; an open-source implementation fallback is defined.
- **Colour direction:** Ink, parchment and roasted mineral tones with clay as a controlled commercial accent. Brown is structural, not decorative.

Fictional canon introduced here should remain consistent in all later product, location and case-study content.

## 1. Brand DNA

### Mission

Make exceptional seasonal coffee easier to choose, brew and enjoy.

### Vision

A coffee culture where knowledge increases pleasure rather than status—where traceability, flavour and good brewing feel understandable to anyone curious enough to begin.

### Brand promise

Every ALDER coffee will tell the customer what it tastes like, why it tastes that way and how to brew it well, in language they can use.

### Positioning

For quality-seeking home brewers who want more from coffee without the ceremony, ALDER is an independent specialty roaster that combines carefully sourced seasonal lots with calm, practical guidance. Unlike brands that perform expertise or hide behind lifestyle imagery, ALDER makes quality legible from first glance to final cup.

### Emotional positioning

The customer should feel **quietly capable**. ALDER is not selling admission to an expert club. It is giving someone the confidence to make a better choice and enjoy the result.

Desired emotional sequence:

1. **Drawn in** by sensory atmosphere.
2. **Oriented** by clear hierarchy.
3. **Reassured** by transparent product information.
4. **Capable** when configuring and brewing.
5. **Rewarded** by the ritual and taste.

### Personality

| Trait     | Expression                                         | Guardrail                                      |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Assured   | Specific recommendations and decisive editing      | Never boastful or award-dependent              |
| Observant | Notices material, season, place and taste          | Never poetic at the expense of meaning         |
| Generous  | Shares knowledge plainly                           | Never patronising or over-explanatory          |
| Patient   | Allows space and considered pacing                 | Never slow, vague or obstructive               |
| Exacting  | Consistent product facts and careful details       | Never clinical or perfectionist theatre        |
| Grounded  | Natural materials, real work and believable people | Never rustic cosplay or manufactured nostalgia |

If ALDER were a person entering a room, it would arrive on time, notice the light, make one good recommendation and let the conversation breathe.

### Brand values

1. **Clarity over performance.** Expertise exists to help the customer.
2. **Care made visible.** Claims are supported by process, detail or evidence.
3. **Seasonality over endless choice.** A small, changing range is a strength.
4. **Respect without mythology.** Producers and places are described specifically, not romanticised.
5. **Practice over perfection.** Better brewing comes through useful guidance, not rigid rules.
6. **Restraint with purpose.** Every visual and functional element earns its place.
7. **Hospitality in every state.** Errors, empty states and policies receive the same care as the hero.

### What makes ALDER different

ALDER turns the complete coffee chain—place, process, roast, brew and taste—into one continuous, understandable story. Many specialty brands possess this information but distribute it across technical product copy, separate education pages and dense subscription terms. ALDER makes it a single decision system.

### The memorable core idea

**Quietly exact** is the memory structure. Public expressions can include:

- “Coffee, made clear.” — strongest launch proposition.
- “Seasonal coffee. Considered roasting.” — useful descriptive line.
- “Find your next good cup.” — useful commerce invitation.

Avoid making “Craft roasted. Slow perfected.” the final line. “Slow” is not inherently a customer benefit, “perfected” is an unverifiable absolute, and the phrasing feels like category advertising. It remains a tonal starting point only.

### If ALDER disappeared tomorrow

Customers would miss a roaster that made interesting coffee feel easy to approach: the concise flavour guidance, reliable brew instructions, small seasonal edit and sense that someone knowledgeable had already removed the noise.

## 2. Brand narrative

### Fictional origin

ALDER began in 2018 in Petaling Jaya, inside the back half of a former joinery workshop. Its founders had worked on opposite sides of coffee: one in roasting and green buying, the other in hospitality and editorial design. Both were frustrated by the same gap. Excellent coffees were arriving in Malaysia, but the language around them often made a simple pleasure feel like a test.

They started with three coffees and a weekly public tasting. The format was deliberately plain: taste first, then explain what in the variety, processing and roast might have created that experience. Customers did not need to identify obscure flavour notes or use the right vocabulary. They only needed to notice what they enjoyed.

The name ALDER came from the workshop's old timber racks, retained when the space was converted. It suggests usefulness, material warmth and steady construction without becoming a literal tree-themed identity.

Today, the fictional company roasts a compact seasonal range and operates one roastery and tasting room. It works through established import and producer relationships, publishes a practical recipe for every coffee and changes the range when a harvest ends rather than maintaining the illusion of permanent availability.

### Why it exists

ALDER exists because quality is more valuable when people can recognise and use it. Sourcing detail, roasting control and brew knowledge should shorten the distance between a producer's work and a customer's enjoyment—not create another layer of prestige.

### Why people trust it

- It is specific about origin, variety, process, roast intent and brew advice.
- It distinguishes verified facts from interpretation.
- It explains recurring prices, shipping and availability before commitment.
- It keeps the range edited and admits when a coffee is gone.
- It does not invent reviews, impact figures, certifications or “direct trade” claims.
- Its guidance remains consistent across packaging, product pages and brew guides.

### What ALDER believes about coffee

- Taste matters more than terminology.
- Good information should increase curiosity, not anxiety.
- Roast style should respond to the coffee and intended brew—not a house ideology imposed on every lot.
- Freshness is a window, not a countdown designed to create urgency.
- Producers deserve specific, contemporary representation rather than pastoral mythology.
- A recipe is a useful starting point, not a commandment.

### What it refuses to become

- A lifestyle label with coffee as a prop.
- A trophy cabinet of awards and scores.
- An endless catalogue built around manufactured scarcity.
- A faux-scientific experience that confuses complexity with credibility.
- A rustic “handcrafted” cliché.
- A content machine publishing thin articles to satisfy a calendar.

### Short brand story

ALDER is an independent roastery in Petaling Jaya built around one simple belief: better coffee should become clearer, not more complicated. We roast a small seasonal range, explain each coffee in language people can use and publish a practical recipe for every bag. The work is exacting. The experience is not.

## 3. Visual direction

### Overall aesthetic: editorial workshop

The design combines the clarity of a well-edited field journal with the tactile atmosphere of a working roastery. “Editorial” supplies hierarchy, pacing and intelligent juxtaposition. “Workshop” supplies material truth, human scale and evidence of making.

This is not minimalism as absence. It is **edited richness**: few elements, high content value, strong image detail and visible functional structure.

### Material inspiration

- Charred and oiled timber, used as tonal references rather than literal backgrounds.
- Uncoated paper, blind embossing and ink absorbed into fibre.
- Glazed stoneware with irregular but controlled surfaces.
- Brushed steel and oxidised roasting hardware.
- Cotton canvas, coffee parchment and marked shipping sacks.
- Crop/lot notation, roast curves and handwritten cupping observations used sparingly as evidence.

Avoid burlap overlays, coffee-ring graphics, beans scattered around products, botanical line drawings and vintage stamp motifs. They signal category before they signal ALDER.

### Lighting style

Directional window light or one large softened source, usually from the side. Blacks retain detail; highlights feel warm but not orange. Hard morning light can appear in documentary scenes, while product still life uses soft fall-off and deliberate shadow.

The reason is sensory: side light reveals paper tooth, ceramic glaze, bean surface and steam. Flat high-key light makes the product clean but removes material desire.

### Editorial direction

- Alternate sensory immersion with structured information.
- Use short, decisive headlines followed by compact explanatory copy.
- Let captions, lot data and process notes function as a secondary reading layer.
- Use pull quotes rarely and only when the words carry evidence or belief.
- Treat price, weight, cadence and shipping as editorially important, not visual clutter.

### Composition philosophy

One dominant subject per frame or section. Asymmetry creates movement, but alignment creates trust. The system should support off-centre images, narrow text measures, edge-aligned captions and occasional full-bleed interruption without turning every section into a different art direction experiment.

### Negative space

White space is functional separation and pacing. It should clarify which product, statement or action matters. It is not a luxury signal by itself and must not force excessive scrolling or bury commercial information.

### Cropping

- Product packs: mostly complete silhouette or intentional close detail; never accidentally clipped.
- People: crop at natural joints and preserve gesture/context; avoid anonymous “hands only” as the default representation of labour.
- Process: close enough to reveal material action, wide enough to understand place.
- Origin: documentary rather than postcard; avoid generic drone landscapes and romantic poverty imagery.
- Use occasional radical macro crops for beans, paper and ceramic as texture fields, never as the only view of a product.

### Texture usage

Texture belongs inside photography and physical assets first. Digital grain may be applied at 1–2% opacity to large quiet surfaces only after compression testing. It must never reduce text contrast, create visible tiling or inflate page weight.

### Background treatments

- Parchment is the default reading surface.
- Ink and Espresso create deliberate chapter changes, not an alternating stripe pattern.
- Oat separates utility regions and secondary information.
- Photography may bleed to the edge when immersion is the goal; commerce controls retain a stable, quiet surface.
- Gradients are not a brand device. A photographic vignette or natural shadow is acceptable; decorative UI gradients are not.

Colour must feel drawn from roasting, paper, mineral glaze and tropical shade—not from a generic “earth tone” palette. The neutral system carries most layouts; Clay indicates controlled action; Moss and Brass are contextual, not competing brand accents.

### Usage ratios

- 60–70% Parchment/Paper.
- 15–20% photography.
- 10–15% Ink/Espresso.
- Under 5% Clay, Moss and Brass combined in most views.

These are compositional guides, not quotas.

### Colour rules

- Never encode flavour, roast, stock or validation by colour alone.
- Clay with inverted text passes normal-text contrast; Brass does not and is never used as a small-text foreground on light surfaces.
- Product families may use muted derived colours on packaging, but interface meaning remains stable.
- Dark mode is not part of the product. Dark editorial sections provide contrast without maintaining a second semantic theme.
- Every colour pairing must be verified in implementation; the documented palette is a starting contract, not a substitute for testing.

## 5. Typography system

Typography must feel authored but never precious. One expressive family creates identity; one quiet family carries every decision, price and instruction.

### Recommended licensed pairing

**Ivar Headline** by Letters from Sweden + **Untitled Sans** by Klim Type Foundry.

- **Hero/display:** Ivar Headline Regular, with Medium available for smaller display sizes. Its sturdy, literary construction feels editorial and warm without resembling decorative café lettering.
- **Section display:** Ivar Headline for major chapter statements and selected product names.
- **Body/UI:** Untitled Sans Regular and Medium. Its intentionally ordinary neo-grotesk character lets content and photography lead.
- **Numeric/data:** Untitled Sans with tabular numerals for prices, quantities, timers and comparison data.
- **Italic:** Ivar Headline Italic for rare captions or editorial emphasis; never long body copy.

Both families offer web formats. Actual production use requires valid web licences and a limited subset. Ivar is not used for functional controls or dense specifications.

### Open-source implementation fallback

If commercial licences are not purchased, use **Newsreader** for display and **Instrument Sans** for body/UI. This is a fallback, not permission to approximate the licensed fonts using unlicensed files. The design must be rechecked because line breaks, x-height and tone will change.

### Hierarchy

| Style      | Desktop target       | Mobile target       | Typeface         | Guidance                                              |
| ---------- | -------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Display XL | 88–112px / 0.92–0.98 | 48–64px / 0.96–1.02 | Ivar             | Hero only; 6–10 words preferred                       |
| Display L  | 64–80px / 0.98       | 40–52px / 1.0       | Ivar             | Major editorial chapters                              |
| Heading 1  | 48–64px / 1.02       | 36–44px / 1.05      | Ivar             | Page title                                            |
| Heading 2  | 36–44px / 1.08       | 28–36px / 1.1       | Ivar             | Section title                                         |
| Heading 3  | 24–30px / 1.15       | 22–26px / 1.18      | Ivar or Untitled | Context decides editorial vs utility                  |
| Lead       | 20–24px / 1.45       | 18–21px / 1.45      | Untitled         | Introductory explanation                              |
| Body       | 17–18px / 1.55–1.65  | 16–18px / 1.55–1.65 | Untitled         | 55–70 characters per line                             |
| Small      | 14–15px / 1.45       | 14–15px / 1.45      | Untitled         | Supporting content, never core legal terms below 14px |
| Label      | 12–13px / 1.2        | 12–13px / 1.2       | Untitled Medium  | Slight letter spacing; sentence case preferred        |

Use fluid `clamp()` sizing between tested bounds. Exact values will be resolved during component work based on real copy, not preserved as poster-like numbers at all costs.

### Typographic rules

- Sentence case for navigation, buttons and labels. All caps is reserved for short lot codes or eyebrow labels.
- Display tracking is slightly negative; body tracking remains normal.
- Never justify text.
- Paragraph measure is 55–70 characters; product specifications may be narrower.
- Use real typographic quotation marks, en dashes and non-breaking price/unit pairs.
- Prices are clear and stable; superscript cents are avoided.
- No more than two weights per family in the initial build.
- Serif creates voice. Sans carries decisions. Do not reverse those roles for novelty.

## 6. Spacing and layout system

### Base rhythm

Use a 4px base with a deliberately sparse semantic scale:

`4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 160`

Components use the lower half; sections use 64–160px fluidly. Avoid arbitrary values unless optical alignment requires a documented exception.

### Grid

- **Wide desktop:** 12 columns, 24px gutters, max shell 1440px.
- **Standard desktop/laptop:** 12 columns, 20–24px gutters.
- **Tablet:** 8 columns, 20px gutters.
- **Mobile:** 4 columns, 16px gutters.
- Grid is an alignment framework, not a requirement that every module occupy a card-shaped rectangle.

### Containers

| Container | Maximum | Purpose                                        |
| --------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Shell     | 1440px  | Header and large visual compositions           |
| Content   | 1280px  | Catalogue and standard sections                |
| Reading   | 720px   | Guides and long-form prose                     |
| Compact   | 560px   | Forms, checkout steps and focused explanations |

### Outer margins

- 16px at the narrowest mobile width.
- 24px on large mobile/tablet.
- 40–64px on desktop depending on viewport.
- Never let a max-width container create tiny gutters on intermediate widths.

### Section rhythm

- Hero to next content: 64–112px.
- Major chapter: 96–160px desktop; 64–96px mobile.
- Related modules: 64–96px.
- Component internal spacing: 12–32px.
- Headline to explanatory copy: 16–24px.
- Copy to action: 24–32px.

Large spacing must correspond to a change in subject or pace. Repeated enormous gaps are not “breathing”; they are lost orientation.

### Alignment

- Text aligns to a clear grid edge even when imagery breaks the grid.
- Product lists prioritise consistent baselines and image ratios.
- Editorial pages may offset captions or secondary facts by one column.
- Do not centre long text. Centred composition is reserved for brief pauses or statements under roughly three lines.

## 7. Component language

This section defines behaviour and character, not component designs.

### Global navigation

Quiet, shallow and highly legible. The wordmark anchors the left or centre depending on breakpoint, while primary commerce paths remain visible. It may transition from transparent over a suitable hero to Parchment after scroll, but only if contrast is guaranteed. Menus use simple panels and clear grouping, not an oversized lifestyle mega-menu.

### Buttons and links

- Primary button: solid Clay or Ink, modest 2–4px corner softening, strong text contrast.
- Secondary button: transparent with a defined Ink border.
- Tertiary action: text link with directional underline movement or arrow.
- Height 44–52px depending on context; padding communicates importance, not pill shape.
- Icon-only controls are square or circular only where the symbol is universally understood and labelled accessibly.
- Hover changes are tonal and positional by 1–2px at most; layout never jumps.

### Coffee cards

Cards are image-and-type compositions, not floating containers. Default treatment has no enclosing border, radius or shadow. Product name, origin, short taste description and price form a stable hierarchy. Availability and subscription information appear only when relevant. Image ratio is consistent within a listing; packaging colour provides variation.

### Product gallery

Large, stable image stage plus thumbnails or index. Mix pack clarity, material detail and contextual brewing. Controls remain visible and keyboard operable. No endless carousel, parallax or hover-only alternate imagery.

### Section headers

An optional compact eyebrow, expressive serif heading and concise sans explanation. Heading/action alignment may be split across columns. Repeated identical centred stacks are avoided.

### Taste profile

Use two human-readable axes—**bright ↔ comforting** and **delicate ↔ full**—plus explicit tasting notes and brew-fit labels. Visual position is supplemental; text always states the result. Avoid radar charts, flavour wheels and false scientific precision.

### Filter controls

Rectangular text controls with restrained rounding, visible count and strong selected state. “Chips” must behave like controls, not decorative tags. On mobile they live in a labelled filter sheet with applied-count feedback and a persistent results action.

### Cart drawer

A calm review surface, not a conversion ambush. Clear line items, editable quantity, purchase cadence and subtotal; one strong checkout action. It returns focus correctly, closes with Escape and always offers the full cart page. Cross-sells are absent in MVP.

### Subscription configurations

Plans read like editorial service choices, then become structured controls. The active choice gains border, surface and checkmark—not colour alone. A persistent summary explains quantity, grind, cadence, next dispatch and recurring total in plain language.

### Footer

An Espresso field that functions as a final index: commerce, learning, visit, policies and the fictional-project disclosure. Newsletter content is concise and inline. It should feel conclusive, not like another promotional page.

### Inputs and forms

Labels remain visible above fields. Fields use Paper on light surfaces, 1px default borders and a distinct 2px focus ring. Helper and error messages reserve space where practical. Required status is textual. Checkout fields are grouped by user task, not database schema.

### Borders, radii and shadows

- Default radius: 2–4px.
- Functional sheets/dialogs: 4–8px when separation benefits from softness.
- Pills: only tags, status or binary segmented controls whose form communicates grouping.
- Shadows: low-opacity, wide and rare; overlays use a shadow plus border. Product cards do not float.
- Dividers and surface shifts are preferred over decorative containers.

## 8. Interaction philosophy

Motion should communicate continuity, causality or state. Atmosphere alone is insufficient justification.

### Timing and easing

| Motion                | Duration  | Easing                    | Purpose                                   |
| --------------------- | --------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Hover/focus feedback  | 120–180ms | ease-out                  | Confirm interactivity                     |
| Control/state change  | 180–240ms | ease-out                  | Connect action to result                  |
| Drawer/sheet          | 240–320ms | decelerating cubic-bezier | Preserve spatial origin                   |
| Image/content reveal  | 400–650ms | gentle ease-out           | Establish editorial pacing                |
| Page-level continuity | 200–350ms | opacity/short transform   | Reduce abruptness, never delay navigation |

Use one shared easing family. Springs are reserved for direct manipulation, not general decoration.

### Rules

- Maximum meaningful translation is usually 8–16px.
- Do not animate layout dimensions when transform/opacity can express the change.
- Hover effects never hide essential information or apply only to pointer users.
- Scroll-triggered reveals occur once, preserve reading order and never make content initially inaccessible.
- Product image changes crossfade or use a restrained clip; no cinematic zoom on every card.
- Cart feedback originates from the action and ends at a visible cart state; avoid novelty “flying product” animations.
- Page transitions must not block browser navigation or create blank intermediate states.
- Reduced-motion mode removes transforms, parallax and staged delays while keeping instantaneous state feedback.

### Loading states

Prefer real server-rendered content and reserved image geometry. Skeletons are used only when structure is known and latency is expected. Skeletons match the final layout, use a static tonal pulse under reduced motion and never simulate content that may not appear.

### Scroll behaviour

Native scrolling is the baseline. No smooth-scroll hijacking. Sticky product summaries and filter bars are allowed only after testing collision, zoom and small-height viewports.

### Micro-interactions

- Button press: subtle tonal shift and 1px compression.
- Filter application: result count updates and focus/announcement confirms the change.
- Grind or cadence change: summary values transition without visual drift.
- Add to cart: button state confirms success, cart count updates and live-region copy is concise.
- Form validation: appears near the field after meaningful interaction or submit, never while the user is mid-entry without reason.

## 9. Photography direction

Photography is the primary emotional medium and must be commissioned, licensed or generated as one coherent body of work. A folder of unrelated premium stock images will break the brand faster than imperfect typography.

### Visual thesis

**Observed craft, not staged craft.** Images should feel composed by someone patient enough to notice work, texture and light, but not arranged to manufacture authenticity.

### Camera and lens feel

- Full-frame or equivalent natural perspective.
- 35–50mm feel for place and people: present, human, not distorted.
- 70–100mm macro/short telephoto for packaging, beans, extraction and material detail.
- Avoid habitual ultra-wide interiors and compressed luxury-product telephoto everywhere.

### Depth of field

Moderate and intentional. Product identity, gesture or material must remain legible. Very shallow focus is reserved for one detail when surrounding context is already established. A whole catalogue of blurred backgrounds would feel cosmetic rather than documentary.

### Lighting

- Natural side light, open shade or one large diffuse source.
- Directional hard light may express morning heat, architecture or machinery.
- Retain texture in dark surfaces; avoid crushed “moody” blacks.
- Steam is used when naturally present, not as a visual effect in every cup image.

### Composition

- One visual subject, clear geometry and enough context to understand action.
- Alternate wide environmental, medium process and close material frames.
- Include people as knowledgeable participants, not lifestyle decoration.
- Leave deliberate negative space for editorial cropping only in selected campaign images.
- Avoid centring every package; slight tension and off-axis placement feel more observed.

### Editing and grade

- Warm-neutral white balance; parchment remains cream, not yellow.
- Muted saturation with selective natural colour retained in fruit, ceramic and packaging.
- Gentle contrast, lifted only enough to preserve paper and shadow detail.
- Skin tones remain accurate across the full range of subjects.
- Grain resembles fine 35mm stock at low intensity; it must survive responsive compression without becoming noise.
- Consistent black point and highlight roll-off across the set.

### Required shot families

1. **Product truth:** front, reverse, scale, seal and bean appearance.
2. **Sensory detail:** bloom, extraction, crema, steam, ground coffee and ceramic.
3. **Roastery work:** weighing, sample roasting, cupping, profiling and packing.
4. **Place:** the Petaling Jaya workshop, filtered tropical light, material details and exterior context.
5. **People:** founders, roasting/packing team and customers learning without staged laughter.
6. **Origin:** producer-supplied or properly commissioned documentary imagery with names, place and context.
7. **Ritual:** quiet home brewing in believable Malaysian interiors, not generic Scandinavian kitchens.

### Image ratios

- Product listing: consistent 4:5.
- Product gallery: 4:5 and 1:1 mix within a stable viewer.
- Editorial feature: 3:2 or 16:10.
- Documentary portrait: 4:5 or 2:3.
- Full-bleed chapter image: flexible crop with a documented safe area.

### Rights and authenticity

Maintain an asset register with creator/source, licence, permitted channels, attribution needs, generation disclosure and crop restrictions. AI-generated origin/producer documentary imagery is prohibited because it would imply real people and places. Generated still-life imagery may be used in the fictional case study if disclosed and visually reviewed for packaging/text errors.

## 10. Iconography

- Outline icons, 1.5px stroke at a 24px base grid.
- Square line caps and gently rounded joins; this mirrors the precise-but-not-harsh identity.
- Optical size set: 16px for dense utility, 20px default, 24px for touch controls.
- Geometric construction with minimal detail. Do not mix emoji, filled icons and outline icons.
- Corner geometry follows function, not the button radius.
- Use familiar symbols for search, cart, close, menu, increment and direction. Custom metaphors require text.
- Filled version is reserved for selected/favourited state if such a feature is later built.
- Icons never replace important text such as “Grind,” “Delivery” or “Subscription.”
- Custom coffee-method pictograms may be developed later as a small family, using the same stroke and view box. They must depict recognisable equipment, not abstract lifestyle marks.

## 11. Design principles

1. **Precision and warmth must coexist.** Every exact data point should sit in an experience that feels human; every atmospheric moment must lead back to useful truth.
2. **Photography creates desire; typography creates trust.** Images carry sensory emotion while type carries identity, price, origin and choice without competition.
3. **One visual focus per section.** A section may sell a product, explain a principle or immerse the customer in place—not all three at equal volume.
4. **Information earns hierarchy.** Size reflects decision importance, not content type conventions or a desire to make everything impressive.
5. **White space marks a change in thought.** Space separates meaning and actions; it is not a luxury decoration or an excuse for long pages.
6. **Show evidence before claims.** A producer name, roast intention or shipping date is stronger than “meticulous,” “ethical” or “fresh.”
7. **Editorial does not mean obscure.** Asymmetry and unusual crops are welcome; navigation, price and controls remain conventional enough to use immediately.
8. **Products do not need boxes to feel organised.** Grid, alignment, imagery and type create structure before borders, cards or shadows are introduced.
9. **Colour is scarce and therefore meaningful.** Accent colour points to action or context; photography supplies most variation.
10. **Motion traces cause and effect.** If an animation cannot explain origin, destination, hierarchy or state, remove it.
11. **Materiality starts with real material.** Paper tooth, ceramic glaze and timber belong in imagery and packaging; digital texture is a supporting trace.
12. **Mobile is a distinct composition.** Preserve priority and reading rhythm rather than stacking desktop columns mechanically.
13. **Every product tells one continuous story.** Place, process, roast, taste and brew should connect across catalogue, detail, cart and guidance.
14. **Accessibility belongs to the aesthetic.** Focus, contrast, labels, motion preferences and error clarity are designed states, not compliance overlays.
15. **Restraint is tested, not assumed.** Remove elements only when comprehension and orientation remain strong; sparseness is not automatically elegant.

## 12. UX principles

### Navigation philosophy

Orient before inspiring. Shop, Subscriptions, Our Story and Brew Guides remain stable primary paths. Search and Cart are predictable utilities. The header may be visually quiet, but labels and focus states are never hidden to preserve a composition.

### CTA hierarchy

One primary action per decision region, one supporting action when needed and text links for exploration. “Shop coffee” beats vague “Discover.” Destructive or irreversible actions never share the primary visual treatment.

### Product discovery

Customers may enter through taste, brew method or a specific coffee. Taste language begins with familiar character—bright, comforting, delicate, full—then progressively reveals origin/process detail. Filters must reduce uncertainty, not demonstrate taxonomy.

### Reading rhythm

Use alternating modes: scan, choose, read, breathe. Long-form pages have summaries, descriptive subheads, clear measures and contextual product links. Important information is not hidden in accordions solely to make the page look shorter.

### Checkout philosophy

The demonstration checkout is calm, linear and explicit. Show what the user is paying, which items recur, when they dispatch and what happens next. Validate in plain language, preserve entered values and never collect real payment details.

### Error handling

State what happened, what remains safe and the next useful action. Keep technical detail out of customer copy but retain diagnostic context for development. Errors appear beside their source and, on submit, in a navigable summary.

### Empty and zero states

An empty cart offers a direct return to the seasonal range. Zero filtered results preserve selected filters, explain the conflict and offer clear-all. Search distinguishes no result from system failure. Sold-out products remain informative and recommend a genuinely similar alternative.

### Accessibility mindset

Design for keyboard, touch, screen reader, zoom, low vision, reduced motion and cognitive clarity at the same time as visual composition. Native controls come first. Alternative text, focus order, announcements and error recovery are part of the interaction specification. Decorative sophistication never depends on an inaccessible custom control.

### Trust and disclosure

The footer, checkout and portfolio case study clearly identify ALDER as fictional. The site contains no fabricated reviews, awards, customer counts or real producer impact claims. Premium trust comes from consistency and transparency, not social-proof theatre.

### Competitive white space

ALDER should occupy the space between maximal coffee spectacle and austere specialist minimalism:

1. **Quiet confidence with strong usability.** Cinematic but immediately shoppable.
2. **Taste as one system.** The same accessible taste coordinates guide cards, filters, product details and subscriptions.
3. **One continuous coffee story.** Place → process → roast → taste → brew is visible as a connected chain.
4. **Subscription transparency as premium service.** Timing and recurring cost are part of hospitality.
5. **A Malaysian material context.** Tropical light, adaptive workshop architecture and local home rituals replace imported Nordic/Pacific Northwest shorthand.
6. **Tactility beyond kraft paper.** Glaze, metal, roast markings, ink and timber create depth without rustic costume.
7. **Editorial content that assists purchase.** Guides and sourcing notes appear where they answer a product question, not inside an isolated “content hub.”

### Competitive guardrails

- Never replicate a competitor's grid, packaging logic, navigation structure, icon family or signature animation.
- Do not adopt Aesop-like apothecary language or Onyx-like ornament to borrow credibility.
- Revisit these sites during visual review to detect accidental convergence.
- Competitive sources show current execution, not timeless truth; ALDER's principles must remain valid if those sites redesign.

## 16. System governance

### Decision hierarchy

When two design choices compete, decide in this order:

1. Customer comprehension and task completion.
2. Accessibility and trust.
3. Brand coherence.
4. Performance.
5. Visual novelty.

Novelty never overrides the first four.

### Token governance

The implementation phase will translate this document into semantic tokens rather than copying raw values throughout components. Expected groups are colour, type, space, size, border, radius, shadow, layer, motion and container. Components should consume semantic roles such as `text-primary` and `surface-page`, not palette names such as `brown-900`.

### Exceptions

An exception is acceptable when it improves a specific composition or interaction and still respects accessibility and performance. It should be local, documented and not silently become a new token. Three repeated exceptions indicate the system needs revision.

### Review checklist

For every future page or component, ask:

- Is the primary thought or action obvious?
- Does precision feel welcoming rather than technical?
- Is ALDER recognisable without the logo?
- Are price, recurrence, availability and next steps explicit?
- Does photography show something true and useful?
- Does the layout rely on card containers or decoration unnecessarily?
- Are type roles consistent?
- Is accent colour scarce and meaningful?
- Does motion explain state or continuity?
- Does the experience work with keyboard, zoom and reduced motion?
- Is mobile composed rather than merely stacked?
- Is the fictional nature disclosed where trust requires it?
